5 Underrated Anime You Need to Watch This Season

Home / Anime & Manga / 5 Underrated Anime
By the Pixel & Parsec team · 8 min read · Updated 2026

Every season the algorithm pushes the same five shows into your recommendations, and every season a handful of genuinely great series slip through the cracks because they didn't get the marketing budget. I keep a running list of the ones I had to find myself — through forums, through a friend's "you have to watch this" text, through pure stubborn scrolling. These five are current standouts that deserved a bigger conversation than they got.

None of these are obscure for obscurity's sake. They're just quieter than the shows dominating your feed, and quieter doesn't mean weaker — in a few cases, it means the opposite.

The watchlist

1. 86 EIGHTY-SIX — the slow-burn mecha drama that trusts its silence

Most mecha shows front-load spectacle. 86 EIGHTY-SIX holds back, spending its first arc on the mundane logistics of a war nobody wanted, before earning its action in a way that actually lands emotionally. If you bounced off giant robot shows because they felt hollow, this is the corrective — the Republic's silent treatment of its "Eighty-Six" soldiers is the real horror of the show, not the mechs themselves.

Where to stream: Crunchyroll (subbed and dubbed); availability has shifted region to region over the past year, so check your library before assuming it's there.

2. Aggretsuko — the slice-of-life with an unexpectedly sharp edge

Billed as a cozy office comedy about a death-metal-screaming red panda, Aggretsuko sneaks in some of the most honest writing about burnout I've seen in the genre. It's funny in the way real coworkers are funny, and it never oversells its emotional beats — the karaoke-rage-room sequences are cathartic in a way that's hard to explain until you've had that exact Tuesday.

Where to stream: Netflix, all four seasons.

3. Erased — the horror-adjacent mystery that respects its audience

No jump scares, no filler — just a tightly plotted time-travel mystery that trusts you to keep up. Erased (Boku dake ga Inai Machi) follows a man who keeps getting thrown back into his own childhood to prevent a kidnapping, and the sound design alone does more heavy lifting than most horror anime manage with a full arsenal of tricks.

Where to stream: Crunchyroll; the full 12-episode season is also on Netflix and Hulu in some regions, so check whichever service you already pay for first.

4. Ping Pong the Animation — the sports anime for people who don't watch sports anime

The training-arc formula is all here, but Masaaki Yuasa's character writing is what elevates Ping Pong the Animation — rivalries that feel earned, a cast that actually talks to each other like people, and animation that treats a single serve like it's the climax of the show. Eleven episodes, zero filler.

Where to stream: Crunchyroll; a great weekend binge since the whole season clocks in under four hours.

5. The Apothecary Diaries — the fantasy/mystery adaptation that finally gets the pacing right

Adaptations of dense light-novel source material usually rush or bloat. The Apothecary Diaries (Kusuriya no Hitorigoto) threads the needle — a poison-obsessed apothecary solves court mysteries in a fictional imperial palace, and the show trims the right side content while keeping the worldbuilding that matters.

Where to stream: Crunchyroll, seasons 1 and 2; season 3 has been announced.
