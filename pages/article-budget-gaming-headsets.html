Best Budget Gaming Headsets Under $100

Home / Gaming & Tech / Budget Gaming Headsets
By the Pixel & Parsec team · 8 min read · Updated 2026

You don't need to spend $250 on a headset to hear footsteps clearly or sound decent on a party call. The sub-$100 bracket has gotten genuinely good over the last few years, and the picks below were chosen specifically because they don't feel like compromises — they feel like headsets that happen to cost less.

I tested for three things that matter more than spec-sheet numbers: clamping pressure after two hours (a headset that hurts by hour three is a bad headset regardless of drivers), mic clarity on a live voice call rather than a quiet recording booth, and whether the sound stage actually helps with positional audio in competitive games.

If positional audio is your top priority, look for headsets that emphasize a wider soundstage over bass-heavy tuning — footstep and reload cues get muddied when a headset is tuned for movie explosions instead of directional clarity.

What to skip regardless of price

Avoid headsets whose product listing photos are the only place they look good. Check for real buyer photos and read the one-and-two-star reviews specifically — that's where clamping-pressure complaints and mic-quality issues show up, since five-star reviews rarely mention long-term comfort.

Console vs. PC compatibility

Not every budget headset works cleanly across platforms. Wired 3.5mm headsets are close to universal, but wireless dongles are often locked to one ecosystem, and USB headsets can behave inconsistently across console generations. If you split time between PC and console, prioritize a headset with a standard 3.5mm jack as a fallback connection option, even if you primarily plan to use a wireless dongle for the low-latency benefit.

Also check whether a headset's companion software (EQ presets, surround sound toggles) is actually necessary to get good sound, or just a nice-to-have. Software-dependent audio tuning can be a problem on console, where you may not have access to install the manufacturer's app at all.

A quick word on mic quality

Budget headset mics vary wildly, and a bad one undoes a lot of the audio-side benefits for anyone playing team-based games with voice chat. Look specifically for a mic with a cardioid or noise-canceling pickup pattern rather than an omnidirectional one — the difference in how much keyboard clatter and background noise gets picked up is significant, even at this price point.

Bottom line: If you only care about one thing, prioritize comfort over sound quality. A headset with slightly worse audio that you can wear for four hours beats one with amazing audio that you take off after ninety minutes.
